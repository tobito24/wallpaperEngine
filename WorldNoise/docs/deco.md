# Decoration

The plan, the properties of an item, the placement passes and the reasons are in [idea.md](../idea.md) ("Decoration"). This doc
describes what exists in the code. Built so far: step 1 (table, loader, dev art), step 2 (terrain block), step 3 (placement and a debug overlay), step 4 (render pass), step 5 (forests, blobs, first tuning), step 6 so far the strips (the cliff ridge), step 7 (fields). Not built: tree rows with crown modules, parts.

- **`src/deco/decoTable.ts`** — `DecoItem` and `DECO_ITEMS`, the one table of all items: `name` (the file stem), `variants` (files
  `<name>-<i>.png`, one file `<name>.png` when 1), `frames`, `pass` (`grove`, `large`, `strip`, `medium`, `small`, `flat`), `layer`, `footprint` and `anchorColumn` (where the
  footprint starts in the sprite, counted from the left on the bottom rows), `surface` (`land` or `water`), `biomes` (weight per biome: eligibility and pool weight),
  optional `grounds`, `clearance`, `repeat`, `overlapsDeco`, `overlapsCliff`. `DECO_CELL_SIZE_BY_PASS` (grove 2, large 6, medium 4, small 2, flat 1) and `DECO_PASS_ORDER` (big to small). The table is
  validated when the module loads (`validateDecoTable`): names are unique, footprint plus clearance fit the cell of the pass, weights are positive, and the biomes match the surface
  (water biomes only for `water` items). Adding an item is one row here plus the PNG; the engine does not change. The first sixteen dev items are the decoration of `InfinityWalker`; the user then made new art (trees for swamp, moss, water, snow, savanna and desert, a snow boulder, snow log and snow plants, desert plants, a skeleton, an oil barrel) which is in the table too: 34 items, 56 files. `fileStem` (optional) names the PNG when it differs from the item name (`log-snow` uses `log-2.png`, `tuft-snow` uses `tuft-small-2.png`: the art of a snow variant is not a variant of the grass item, because a variant is picked in every biome of the item).
- **`src/deco/decoSheets.ts`** — loads `assets/deco/*.png` (`import.meta.glob`, like the grounds): `decoFileName`, `decoImage` (undefined until the image is loaded or when
  the PNG is missing, so a missing file draws nothing), `decoSpriteSize` (the sprite size in tiles is the PNG size divided by 32 and by the frames), `decoFilesMissing`.
- **`scripts/cut_infinitywalker_deco.py`** — cuts the items of `InfinityWalker/img/tileset.png` (cells from `InfinityWalker/js/tiles.js`: flowers, tufts, ferns, stump, rocks, logs,
  boulders, shells, lilies, the oak, the palm) into `assets/deco/`, 31 files. Run from `WorldNoise/` with `python scripts/cut_infinitywalker_deco.py` (needs Pillow). The art is
  not cleared for publishing, so `assets/` stays git-ignored; a clone runs the script once.
- **Terrain block (step 2, done).** The deco reads the terrain through `world/terrainBlock.ts` (see [world-and-render.md](world-and-render.md)), the same function `Chunk` uses, with a margin around the chunk. Measured on 400 chunks (cold = first time, with the road and town caches empty; warm = caches full): a block of the chunk (16 tiles) 14.1 ms cold, 2.2 ms warm; with a margin of 8 tiles (32) 17.0 / 4.6 ms; with a margin of 12 tiles (40) 18.1 / 6.0 ms. So the margin costs about 5 ms per chunk once, when it is created, not per frame. If that is too much the deco is computed per block of 2 x 2 chunks and cached.
- **Sprite size in the table (step 3).** `DecoItem.sprite` states the sprite size in tiles in the table as well as the PNG: the placement must not depend on whether an image has loaded yet. The table is the truth for
  placement, the PNG for drawing; they have to agree (the sixteen dev items were compared with their PNGs: no mismatch).
- **`src/deco/decoHash.ts`** — `decoHash(a, b, c, d)`: a fast integer hash (murmur style, seeded with `SEED`) to a number in [0, 1). The string-keyed `randomValue` makes a new `Alea` per call, too slow for thousands of cells per chunk.
- **`src/deco/decoPlacement.ts`** — `decoInRegion(minX, minY, size, stats?)` and `computeDecoOfChunk(cx, cy)` (cached per region of 2 x 2 chunks). The result is a list of `DecoInstance` (`item`, `variant`, `x`, `y` = top left of the footprint, `length` of a strip, `level`); an item belongs to the region that holds its footprint corner.
  Per region it takes a terrain block with a margin of `DECO_MARGIN_TILES` (31, computed from the table, see `DECO_PASS_EXPANSION_TILES`: the longest chain of links between passes, each as long as the larger reach of the two, plus the reach of the last item; it was 20 with four-tile sprites only and grew with the strips of 8 tiles: three links of the acceptance chain plus the sprite reach) and goes through the passes `grove`, `large`, `strip`, `medium`, `small`, `flat`:
  1. the cells of the pass (6, 4, 2, 1 tiles) in the region expanded by `DECO_PASS_EXPANSION_TILES[pass]` (the longest chain of links to later passes, a link being the larger of `reach(first) + left(second)` and `reach(second) + left(first)`; `left` = the largest `anchorColumn` of a pass, the sprite reaching that far to the left), so later passes need less;
  2. per cell: a hash decides the point in the cell; the pool of the pass and of the ground under the point (`item.biomes`, weights) must exist; the fill probability is `DECO_DENSITY_BY_PASS_AND_BIOME[pass][biome]` (else `DECO_PASS_DENSITY[pass] x DECO_BIOME_FACTOR[biome]`) times the cluster factor (cluster = `1 + DECO_CLUSTER_STRENGTH x (2 noise - 1)`, a fractal noise of frequency `DECO_CLUSTER_FREQUENCY_BY_PASS[pass]`); a hash picks the item by weight and the variant; the footprint is moved so the **sprite stays inside its cell** (for items with `overlapsDeco`, like the oak, the **footprint** stays inside the cell and the sprite may reach out), so candidates of one pass never conflict;
  3. **valid** when every footprint tile has a ground that is a biome of the item, no town info (plateau, pad, landing or ring), no road, no stairs, one level (water level only for water items), no cliff piece (the **inner ring of a cliff is allowed**, decided with the user: the cliffs looked bare); items that are not `flat` also keep one tile from a road and three from stairs; the overhang (sprite minus footprint) covers no town, road or stairs and no cliff unless `overlapsCliff`;
  4. **accepted** when it does not conflict with an accepted item of an earlier pass (the occupancy of a pass is committed only when the pass is done, so `clearance` never decides between two items of one pass, which would depend on the order of the cells and so on the region; `DECO_CONFLICT_REACH_TILES` is the largest sprite dimension plus the largest clearance): footprints do not overlap, not inside the clearance of the other (`clearance` both ways), the footprint is not under the sprite of an item without `overlapsDeco`, and its own sprite does not cover a footprint unless it has `overlapsDeco`.
  `DecoStats` counts candidates, cells without a pool, gated cells, accepted items and the rejection reasons (`ground`, `town`, `road`, `stairs`, `level`, `cliff`, `overhang`, `conflict`).
- **Render pass (step 4).** `src/render/DecoRenderer.ts` is called by `WorldRenderer` after all tiles (ground, cliffs, stairs, town pieces, level shade) and before the debug overlays. It takes the chunks of the view expanded by `DECO_CONFLICT_REACH_TILES` (so a crown from the next chunk is drawn), computes the missing ones nearest to the camera first, at most
  `DECO_REGIONS_PER_FRAME` (1) region of `DECO_REGION_CHUNKS` x `DECO_REGION_CHUNKS` (2 x 2) chunks per frame (`computeDecoOfChunk` computes the whole region and fills the cache of its four chunks, because the terrain margin is large: measured warm, a region of 16 tiles costs 18 ms per chunk, of 32 tiles 26 ms per region (6.6 per chunk), of 64 tiles 49 ms (3 per chunk); `peekDecoOfChunk` only reads the cache), so a new view fills in over a few frames and no frame stalls (the items of a chunk pop in). The instances are sorted once per chunk set and number of computed chunks by `(layer, row of the footprint bottom, column)`; `flat` first, then `object`, then `overhead`
  (no item uses `overhead` yet), and drawn scaled with `drawImage`, culled to the view; `frames` pick the part of the sprite strip by the water animation frame. The **level shade** of the footprint level (`DecoInstance.level`) tints the sprite: `src/deco/decoTint.ts` makes one tinted canvas per image and shade (`source-atop` fill), cached in a `WeakMap`, so transparent parts stay clear. The chunk cache holds `DECO_CACHE_MAX_ENTRIES` (1500) chunks and drops the oldest quarter when full (not the whole map: a full clear made all visible deco vanish and refill); below `DECO_MIN_TILE_SIZE_PX` (8 px per tile) no deco is drawn or computed, so the range of the view stays below the cache size (at 4K and 8 px about 830 chunks). An image that has not loaded yet (or a missing PNG) draws nothing and appears on a later frame.
- **Forests and blobs (step 5).** `DecoPass` has a fifth pass, `grove` (cell 2), before `large`: the oak lives there, with `overlapsDeco`, so its candidate is clamped only by its footprint (a tile row of 2 x 1 in the cell) and its sprite may reach into the neighbour cells; `validateDecoTable` asks the sprite to fit the cell only for items that do not
  `overlapsDeco`. Same-pass candidates still have disjoint footprints, so the acceptance chain is unchanged, the chain is one link longer, and `DECO_MARGIN_TILES` was 20 then (5 passes x 4), see above for the computed value. The pass density is the entry in `DECO_DENSITY_BY_PASS_AND_BIOME` (grove: forest 0.6, jungle 0.5, snow forest 0.5, meadow 0.12, steppe 0.05, savanna 0.03)
  or `DECO_PASS_DENSITY x DECO_BIOME_FACTOR`, times the cluster factor of the pass (`DECO_CLUSTER_FREQUENCY_BY_PASS`, grove 0.02 with the strength 2.2, so forests and clearings). `src/deco/decoBlobs.ts`: `blobsOverlapping` (the blobs of the 40-tile cells around a block) and `blobStrength(blobs, x, y)`
  (0 to 1: the rotated, scaled distance, roughened by an edge noise, through a soft edge of the width `dither`); in `candidateAt` a point of a blob on `DECO_BLOB_BIOMES` takes the pool of the items with `blob: true` and `DECO_BLOB_DENSITY x strength` in the `small` and `flat` passes, and thins out every other pass by `1 - strength`.
  Measured on 144 chunks: two ways to compute one area still agree (1615 items, 0 differences), 0 violations, 0 overlaps; the densest 32 x 32 tiles hold 49 oaks. Checked in the browser: a dense forest with overlapping crowns, and oval patches of dense tufts in steppe and meadow.
- **Strips (step 6, the cliff ridge).** `DecoItem.strip` (`minLength`, `maxLength`) makes an item a row of `length` tiles: a left end, a repeating middle and a right end, one PNG per part (`cliff-ridge-left.png`, `-middle.png`, `-right.png`; `DECO_STRIP_PARTS`, `decoStripImages`), 3 to 8 tiles, in the pass `strip` (cell 10, density 0.5, about one ridge per 8 chunks on the first measure).
  `DecoInstance.length` carries the drawn length; `decoFootprintOf` and `decoSpriteOf` (both exported) give the rectangles. The table of a strip states the maximum (sprite and footprint 8 x 1, anchor 0, one variant) and `validateDecoTable` checks it. `DecoRenderer` draws the pieces one per tile with the level shade.
  Not built yet: tree rows with the crown modules (a strip of whole columns).
- **Fields (step 7).** `DecoItem.area` (`minWidth`, `maxWidth`, `minHeight`, `maxHeight`, at least 3 x 3) with the pass `'field'` makes an item a rectangle of 9 pieces: one 96 x 96 PNG per look (`tall-grass-light.png`, `-dry.png`, `-dark.png`, the 3 x 3 matrix of `InfinityWalker`: corners, edges, centre), drawn tile by tile from the matrix by the position in the rectangle (`DecoRenderer`). The matrix has no inner corners, so only
  rectangles. The look comes from the ground under a random point of the cell (`item.biomes`: meadow light, steppe and savanna dry, forest and jungle dark). Fields are **not** a pass: a field is a pure function of the terrain (`fieldAt`: cells of `DECO_FIELD_CELL_TILES` (12), the chance `DECO_FIELD_CHANCE` (0.85), up to `DECO_FIELD_ATTEMPTS` (8) positions and sizes per cell,
  valid by the same `invalidReason` as an item, all tiles at once, so a field is never cut), so it needs no longer chain: after the passes the items whose footprint touches a field are dropped (`decoInRegion`), so no small item stands in the tall grass; crowns may hang over its edge. The margin did not change (31). Layer `flat`, owner = the region of the top left tile.
  Measured on 400 chunks: 96 fields, 0.24 per chunk (about one per four chunks; only grass biomes), sizes 3 x 3 to 6 x 5, 41 dry, 38 dark, 17 light; 0 items inside a field; two ways to compute one area agree (1798 items, 0 differences).
- **`src/render/decoDebug.ts`** — with the debug mode (`r`) the footprints (cyan) and the sprite rectangles (white) of the accepted items are drawn over the visible chunks.
- **Measured (step 3, 144 chunks, one seed):** two ways to compute the same area agree (a 32 x 32 region at once against four chunks: 1661 items, 0 differences), no item stands on a wrong ground, road, town, stairs tile or on another item (0 violations, 0 overlaps),
  items per 100 tiles by the ground under the footprint corner: meadow 27, forest 29, steppe 23, savanna 37, beach 33, mountain 8, tundra 8, jungle 7, desert 4, shallow water 16, deep water 0.1; 74560 candidates: 41406 without a pool for that ground, 21442 not filled, 6794 accepted, 4918 rejected
  (most by cliff 2426, town 850, conflict 525, ground 406). About 13 ms per chunk including the terrain block (cold, with the measuring code), the block itself being the larger part.
- Sprites are 32 px per tile. The oak and the palm are 4 x 3 tiles with a footprint of 2 x 1 at column 1 (the trunk stands on the two middle tiles of the bottom row).
- **New items (2026-11-17).** Trees in the pass `grove`: `tree-mossy` (forest, jungle), `tree-swamp` (jungle until there is a swamp biome), `tree-water` (shallow water, surface `water`), `tree-oak-snow` (snow forest, tundra, snow field), `tree-savanna`, `tree-cactus` (desert); the grove density per biome got entries for desert 0.03, tundra 0.05, snow field 0.02, shallow water 0.08, savanna 0.05 (a biome without an entry would get the pass density 0.5). `boulder-snow` (large; snow field, tundra, snow peak), `log-snow` (medium; snow forest, tundra), `desert-plant`, `skeleton` and `oil-barrel` (small; the last two rare: weights 0.06 in the desert, the skeleton 0.03 in the savanna), `snow-grass`, `tuft-snow` and `desert-grass` (flat).
  Measured on 196 regions: tree-oak-snow 744, tree-mossy 540, tree-water 367, tree-swamp 199, tree-savanna 91, tree-cactus 70, boulder-snow 55, log-snow 171, desert-plant 388, skeleton 22, oil-barrel 7.
